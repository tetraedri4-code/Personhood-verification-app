# Securing your eID

The app shows this page when it recommends securing your eID: when your enrolment is refused because
someone already enrolled with your identity, when you report "it wasn't me", and when someone asks to
replace the secret on your phone without your knowing.

## Why the eID comes first

The app trusts whoever can log in with your eID **and** holds the app. If someone else can still log
in as you, they can lift a suspension or refuse your replacement again. So close the eID first, get new
means, and only then use the app:

1. **Close (block) the eID means that may have been used**: the login app, ID card certificates,
   mobile ID or bank codes, whichever you use. Your country's page below says how, with phone numbers.
2. **Report the crime to the police.** Using someone else's identity is a crime in every member state.
3. **Get new eID means** from the provider.
4. **In the app:** `pop holder replace --not-me`, logging in with the **new** eID means.

If the suspension is lifted again after you secured your eID, someone still has a way to log in as you:
tell the police and your eID provider.

## You have a right to have it closed

EU rules require every eID scheme notified for cross-border use to make it possible to suspend or
revoke an eID means "in a timely and effective manner" (Commission Implementing Regulation (EU)
2015/1502, Annex, point 2.2.3). National law often says more; your country's page names it where it was
checked.

The country pages were checked on 19–20 November 2026 from the providers' and authorities' own pages,
which are linked. Phone numbers and opening hours change: the linked page is the one to trust. Where
something could not be checked, the page says so.
